If you are filing Form 5472 for a foreign-owned single-member LLC, you also file a "Pro Forma 1120." The term confuses most foreign founders, because Form 1120 is the US corporate income tax return and a single-member LLC is not a corporation.

The short version: the Pro Forma 1120 is a Form 1120 used as a transmittal cover sheet. It computes no tax. It exists to give Form 5472 a return to attach to. Most of it stays blank, and an incomplete one can trigger the same $25,000 penalty as a missing Form 5472, because the IRS treats the two as a single packet.

## Why it is called "pro forma"

"Pro forma" is Latin for "as a formality." In tax practice it signals a document filed to satisfy a procedural requirement rather than to compute a substantive result.

A Pro Forma 1120 has the structure of Form 1120 but is filed without completing the income, deduction, or tax computation sections. Founders who encounter the term often assume the LLC owes corporate tax because Form 1120 is involved. It does not. The "pro forma" designation is precisely what signals that no tax is being computed.

## Why a disregarded entity files a corporate return at all

A foreign-owned single-member LLC is a disregarded entity under Treas. Reg. §301.7701-3. The LLC is not separately a taxpayer; its activities flow through to the foreign owner.

In 2017 the IRS issued Treas. Reg. §301.7701-2(c)(2)(vi), creating a narrow exception: for purposes of §6038A reporting, and only for that purpose, foreign-owned US single-member LLCs are treated as corporations. This brought them inside the §6038A regime that already applied to 25% foreign-owned US corporations.

That regime requires Form 5472 to be attached to a Form 1120. Since the LLC has no income tax to compute, the Form 1120 is filed in pro forma fashion, with identifying information only. The IRS preserved the existing attachment structure so foreign-owned LLCs would route through the same processing channels, without requiring them to compute tax they do not owe.

The practical consequence: the Pro Forma 1120 is a transmittal device, not a tax computation. Founders new to the requirement often spend hours trying to work out what belongs on the income lines. The answer is nothing.

## Which fields you actually complete

Per the IRS Instructions for Form 5472, the only information required on the Pro Forma 1120 is the name and address of the foreign-owned US DE, plus **Items B and E on page 1**. Everything else is left blank.

### Heading, top of page 1

Write **"Foreign-owned U.S. DE"** across the top. This tells the Ogden processing center what kind of filer this is.

If the LLC has a fiscal year other than the calendar year, enter the fiscal year dates on the heading line.

### Name and address block

- **Name of corporation:** the LLC's full legal name, exactly as it appears on the EIN assignment letter and on Form 5472 Part I, Line 1a.
- **Address:** the LLC's US business address, matching Form 5472 Part I. This is typically the registered agent's address or the address on file with your bank.

### Item B: Employer Identification Number

Enter the LLC's EIN exactly as issued. An LLC without an EIN cannot file. Foreign owners apply via Form SS-4 by mail or fax; the online EIN application is not available to applicants without a US taxpayer ID.

### Item E: Initial / Final / Name change / Address change

Check whichever applies:

- **Initial return**, for the first year the LLC files.
- **Final return**, if the LLC is dissolving during the year. Form 5472 is still required for transactions through the dissolution date, and no further filings are needed after that.
- **Name change**, if the LLC's legal name changed since the prior filing.
- **Address change**, if the LLC's address changed since the prior filing.

For an ongoing year with no changes, Item E is left blank.

### Signature block

Sign and date. For most foreign-owned single-member LLCs the foreign owner signs as Owner, Member, or Sole Member, with their full legal name printed. Wet signature or a printed, signed copy. Foreign-owned disregarded entities cannot e-file or sign electronically.

Leave "Paid Preparer Use Only" blank if self-prepared.

## Which fields you leave blank

**Item A** (consolidated return, personal holding company, personal service corporation, Schedule M-3 indicator), **Item C** (date incorporated) and **Item D** (total assets) are not required for a pro forma filing and are left blank.

Some preparers voluntarily enter total assets in Item D to keep it consistent with Form 5472 Line 1c. That is optional, not a filing requirement.

All income lines, deduction lines, tax and payment lines, and every schedule (C, J, K, L, M-1, M-2, M-3) are left blank. The LLC reports no income and computes no tax on this form. If the foreign owner has US-source effectively connected income, that is reported on the owner's Form 1040-NR, not here.

Attaching only page 1 and attaching the full form with schedules blank are both accepted. The IRS instructions do not require omitting them.

## What the Pro Forma 1120 does not do

**It does not compute US corporate tax.** Foreign-owned single-member LLCs are not subject to US corporate tax, because they remain disregarded entities for general tax purposes.

**It does not report business income.** Income, if any, is reported by the foreign owner on Form 1040-NR for effectively connected income, or not at all where the LLC is a transparent vehicle for foreign-source income.

**It does not satisfy any state filing.** Wyoming or Delaware annual reports, franchise taxes and other state obligations are separate and unaffected.

**It does not replace Form 5472.** The Pro Forma 1120 alone does not satisfy §6038A. Form 5472 is the substantive reporting; the pro forma is the cover sheet.

## Where to file

Mail or fax the complete packet, Pro Forma 1120 with Form 5472 attached, to the dedicated address for foreign-owned US DEs:

> Internal Revenue Service, 1973 Rulon White Blvd, M/S 6112, Attn: PIN Unit, Ogden, UT 84201

Fax: 855-887-7737.

Do not use the standard Form 1120 mailing address that applies to traditional US corporations. That routes to a different processing center and can delay or misprocess the return.

E-filing is not available for foreign-owned disregarded entities. Send by certified mail with return receipt, or fax at 300 DPI or higher.

## What this means for your filing

If you are filing for the first time, you are sending two forms as one packet: the Pro Forma 1120 cover sheet and Form 5472. Complete only the name and address block, Item B, Item E where applicable, and the signature. Leave Items A, C and D blank, along with every income, deduction, tax and schedule section.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does my LLC owe corporate tax because Form 1120 is involved?**
No. The Pro Forma 1120 is a cover sheet, not a tax computation. The LLC remains a disregarded entity for income tax purposes and owes no US corporate tax as a result of filing it.

**Can I file Form 5472 without the Pro Forma 1120?**
No. The IRS instructions require Form 5472 to be attached to a Form 1120, filed in pro forma fashion for foreign-owned DEs. Form 5472 alone is incomplete and may be rejected or misprocessed.

**Do I need to fill in total assets or date incorporated?**
No. For foreign-owned US DEs the instructions list only the name and address, Item B and Item E. Some preparers add total assets voluntarily to match Form 5472 Line 1c, but it is not required.

**Is Pro Forma 1120 the same as Form 1120-F?**
No. Form 1120-F is the US Income Tax Return of a Foreign Corporation. A Pro Forma 1120 is a Form 1120 used as a transmittal for Form 5472 by foreign-owned US disregarded entities. Different forms, different purposes.

**Do I write "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" on Form 5472 as well?**
No. It goes on the Pro Forma 1120 page 1 heading. Form 5472 has its own structure identifying the reporting corporation.

**What if I accidentally filled in income on the Pro Forma 1120?**
Filling in income lines does not create a tax liability for a foreign-owned single-member LLC, but it can confuse IRS processing. If you have already filed that way, file an amended Pro Forma 1120 marked "Amended" with the income lines blanked out.

**Do I file a Pro Forma 1120 every year?**
Every year that Form 5472 is filed. The cover sheet accompanies each year's Form 5472. There is no one-time filing.

**What if the LLC is in its final year?**
Check the Final return box under Item E. Form 5472 is still required for the final year, covering transactions through the dissolution date. No further filings are required after dissolution.
